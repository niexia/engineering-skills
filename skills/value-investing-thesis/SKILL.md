# Value Investing Thesis

Produce a decision-oriented thesis in the user's language, plus a summary card
image and an explicit accuracy gate. The image is mandatory on every run, even
when the conclusion is PASS or the data is incomplete.

Read the [accuracy playbook](references/accuracy-playbook.md) before writing.
Accuracy is a gate, not a disclaimer.

## Output contract

1. A research note based on the [report template](assets/report-template.md),
   covering: fact base and provenance, business model, three-layer truth,
   three-lens analysis, normalized earnings, scenario valuation, pre-mortem,
   decision and tracking checklist.
2. `summary.json` following the [summary schema](references/summary-schema.md),
   including a `confidence` grade (A/B/C).
3. `checks.json` with the arithmetic checks, and the output of
   `scripts/check_numbers.py`.
4. `summary.png` generated by `scripts/make_summary_card.py`. Verify that it
   exists, opens as an image, and contains no missing-glyph boxes. For Chinese
   output, pass `"locale": "zh"` and a CJK font path if the default font fails.
5. An explicit `Accuracy gate: PASS | FAIL | VACUOUS` line in the note.
6. Embed the image and link the note, summary JSON and checks JSON.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the fact base

Collect the latest primary sources: annual and interim reports, quarterly
updates, exchange filings, ownership disclosures, buyback and dividend records,
current price, share count and diluted shares. Record the as-of date and currency.

Put this header before any number:

```text
Company:          [name]
Ticker:           [exchange: ticker]
Price as of:      [date, currency]
Market cap:       [value, currency, date]
Enterprise value: [value, currency]
Fiscal year:      [end date; 52/53-week?]
Reporting unit:   [millions/billions; currency]
Share base:       [basic/diluted; weighted average/period-end]
Accounting:       [IFRS/US GAAP/other; lease convention]
```

Minimum data: revenue, gross profit, operating profit, net profit, operating cash
flow, capex, net cash or debt, inventory, receivables, ROE/ROIC, segment and
geographic mix, customer or IP concentration, management ownership, buybacks,
dividends and valuation multiples.

Rules:

- **Source hierarchy**: primary filing > regulator/exchange data > company
  investor materials > reputable press or data vendor > sell-side research >
  social media. A secondary source never overrides a primary filing. Web search
  is a last resort and must be labeled.
- **Freshness**: every figure has an as-of date. Price is as of a trading day;
  consensus is dated; filings are period-labeled. Mark aging, stale, superseded
  or unknown data explicitly.
- **Units and periods**: do not mix currencies, units, fiscal periods or share
  bases. State the lease, minority, pension and associate conventions.
- **Missing data**: missing debt is not zero. Write "not disclosed" or
  `NEEDS SOURCE`, never zero.
- **Conflicts**: when two sources disagree on a material figure, use the
  [conflict register](references/accuracy-playbook.md#5-conflict-register). Do not
  average and do not pick silently.
- **Evidence labels**: every load-bearing claim gets `FACT`, `COMPANY CLAIM`,
  `ESTIMATE`, `MODEL`, `JUDGEMENT` or `ASSUMPTION`, with a source and date.

If a figure is unavailable, write "not disclosed" and continue. Never invent a
number, a quote or a date. Distinguish reported, adjusted and author-estimated
figures. Cite the source and period for every material number.

### 2. Decompose the business

Explain in one paragraph how the company earns cash. Split revenue into drivers
(volume, price, mix, geography, channel) and identify the unit economics.
Separate:

- **Stable core**: repeat or consumable demand, installed base, membership,
  essential use cases, recurring maintenance.
- **Cyclical layer**: channel restocking, macro, fashion, promotions, inventory
  cycle, currency.
- **Bubble layer**: speculative demand, resale premia, one-off events, channel
  stuffing, pull-forward from future periods.

This three-layer split is the core analytical move. State which layer is growing
or shrinking and what evidence supports the classification. A company can look
cheap on peak earnings and expensive on normalized earnings; the split prevents
that mistake.

### 3. Apply the three lenses

Read [the framework](references/framework.md) before scoring. For each lens,
record evidence for and against; do not average away contradictions.

- **Buffett**: circle of competence, moat, owner earnings, capital allocation,
  management integrity, margin of safety.
- **Duan Yongping**: 买股票就是买公司; business-model differentiation and pricing
  power; 本分/平常心/stop doing list; 毛估估; opportunity cost.
- **Munger**: inversion and pre-mortem; psychology and incentives; the "too hard"
  pile; the 100% buy test; avoid stupidity.

Then add:

- **Consensus vs variant perception**: what the market currently believes, what
  you believe differently, why the market may be wrong, and what would prove you
  wrong. If you cannot state a variant perception, the conclusion is "no edge"
  or "watch", not "buy".
- **Ticker-swap test**: replace the company with its closest competitor. If the
  thesis still reads as plausible, it is generic; add company-specific evidence
  or lower the confidence.
- **Strongest bear case**: write it as if you held it. Include the assumption that
  breaks the thesis, the re-rating mechanism, an estimated drawdown, and the
  early warning.

Score five dimensions from 1 to 5: business quality, moat, management, valuation,
risk (5 = best). Show the evidence behind each score. A score without a source is
an opinion.

### 4. Normalize earnings

Estimate mid-cycle owner earnings, not the latest peak or trough. Adjust for
one-offs, FX, stock-based compensation, channel stuffing, inventory build,
maintenance capex and working capital. Show the bridge from reported net profit
to normalized free cash flow. Use a range, not a point estimate. Explain every
adjustment and label it as reported, adjusted or estimated.

Grade the two or three assumptions that do the most work: A (primary evidence,
triangulated), B (one material uncertainty), C (thin evidence, provisional).
Carry the overall grade into `summary.json`.

### 5. Value with scenarios

Build bear, base and bull cases with explicit revenue, margin and multiple
assumptions. Show implied market cap, per-share value and return from the current
price. Compute a rough expected value with stated probabilities. Cross-check with
a reverse DCF: what growth and margin does the current price imply?

State the single assumption doing the most work. Stress the bear case: estimate
the drawdown, not just the direction. Compare with at least one durable peer and
with the opportunity cost of an index or the user's best alternative. Do not
present a single target price as fact.

### 6. Decide and define the rules

Choose one verdict:

- **BUY**: within your circle, moat durable, management trustworthy, price offers
  a margin of safety.
- **WATCH**: good business but price, evidence or a key uncertainty is not right.
- **PASS**: outside the circle, poor business, or a governance/accounting red flag.

State the buy zone, watch zone and avoid zone; position size; position
suitability (core / satellite / option / avoid); expected return; and what would
change the verdict. Define sell rules before buying.

### 7. Run the accuracy gate

Write `checks.json` with the arithmetic checks and run:

```bash
python scripts/check_numbers.py checks.json
```

The checker compares the value used in the analysis with the value you recomputed
from the formula, prints a Markdown table, and ends with `Accuracy gate: PASS`,
`FAIL` or `VACUOUS`. It exits non-zero on failure. Do not publish a conclusion on
`FAIL`; fix the numbers and re-run. If the checker cannot run or has no checks,
state `VACUOUS` and why. An un-run gate that reports success is the failure mode
this step exists to prevent.

Minimum checks: market cap, enterprise value, net cash, P/E, margins, FCF,
per-share value, scenario probabilities, expected return, and bull/base/bear
ordering. The [playbook](references/accuracy-playbook.md#6-arithmetic-checks)
lists the standard formulas and common EV/per-share mistakes.

Complete the checklist in the [report template](assets/report-template.md#10-accuracy-gate)
and put the result in the note.

### 8. Build the summary card

Write `summary.json` from the schema. Run:

```bash
python scripts/make_summary_card.py summary.json --out summary.png
```

The script uses Pillow for PNG. If Pillow is unavailable it writes an SVG and
prints a warning. Verify the output with an image read. If Chinese characters
render as boxes, pass `--font /path/to/CJK-font.ttf` or set `SUMMARY_CARD_FONT`.

The card must contain: company, ticker, as-of date, verdict, confidence, five
scores, key metrics, three-layer truth, bull and bear points, three lens lines
and decision zones. Every metric carries its own period (`FY25`, `1H26`, `TTM`,
`2026E`); the header as-of is not a substitute. Keep bullets short; the script
wraps text and shrinks it within limits, but it does not rewrite content.

### 9. Deliver

Lead with the verdict and the one reason that matters. Then the evidence. Separate
fact, estimate and opinion using the evidence labels. Include the image, the note,
the summary JSON and the checks. On a repeat analysis, compare the prior estimates
with actuals and state the error before updating the thesis. End with the tracking
checklist and "not investment advice."

## Evidence labels

| Label | Use for |
|---|---|
| `FACT` | Reported in a primary document or a closed-period data tool |
| `COMPANY CLAIM` | Guidance, market size or market share the company asserts |
| `ESTIMATE` | Consensus or single-analyst figure, with vintage |
| `MODEL` | Computed from labeled inputs; show formula |
| `JUDGEMENT` | Your view, not a measurement |
| `ASSUMPTION` | Input chosen without evidence; state basis and sensitivity |
| `NEEDS SOURCE` | Required figure still missing; resolve or remove before delivery |

Use the reader-facing wording in the playbook: "the company reports", "consensus
of N analysts as of [date]", "our model implies", "we assume". Do not write
"clearly", "obviously", "will" or "the market is wrong" without evidence.

## Failure handling

- Missing price or share count: mark valuation as unavailable; still produce the
  qualitative thesis and a card with "valuation: n/a". State `VACUOUS` for the
  valuation checks.
- Missing debt or cash: do not treat it as zero; mark the valuation incomplete
  and skip the affected checks.
- Stale price or consensus: refresh it or mark it stale; never present it as
  current.
- Conflicting sources: record both, choose by source tier, state what would
  settle it. Do not average.
- Arithmetic check failure: fix the numbers and re-run. If it still fails, lower
  the confidence and do not present the conclusion as final.
- Missing image tooling: produce the SVG fallback; do not omit the image.
- User asks for a quick take: still produce the card and at least the minimum
  arithmetic checks, but shorten the note.
- User asks for short-term trading or a price prediction: decline that framing and
  offer the long-term thesis instead.

## Quality bar

- Every material claim has a source, a date and an evidence label.
- No invented numbers, quotes or management intentions.
- No mixed currencies, units, periods or share bases.
- The bear case is as strong as the bull case, with a drawdown estimate.
- The thesis passes the ticker-swap test: if it reads the same for a competitor,
  it is generic and needs company-specific evidence.
- The verdict follows from the evidence, not from a personality or a narrative.
- The accuracy gate ran and its result is stated.
- The image is readable at chat size and contains no clipped text.
- The note distinguishes "I know", "I estimate" and "I guess".
