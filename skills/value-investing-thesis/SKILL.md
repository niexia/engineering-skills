# Value Investing Thesis

Produce a decision-oriented thesis in the user's language, plus a summary card
image. The image is mandatory on every run, even when the conclusion is PASS or
the data is incomplete.

## Output contract

1. A research note covering: verdict, fact base, business model, three-layer
   truth, three-lens analysis, normalized earnings, scenario valuation,
   pre-mortem and tracking checklist.
2. `summary.json` following the [summary schema](references/summary-schema.md).
3. `summary.png` generated by `scripts/make_summary_card.py`. Verify that it
   exists, opens as an image, and contains no missing-glyph boxes. For Chinese
   output, pass `"locale": "zh"` and a CJK font path if the default font fails.
4. Embed the image in the response and link the note and JSON.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the fact base

Collect the latest primary sources: annual and interim reports, quarterly
updates, exchange filings, ownership disclosures, buyback and dividend records,
current price, share count and diluted shares. Record the as-of date and currency.

Minimum data: revenue, gross profit, operating profit, net profit, operating cash
flow, capex, net cash or debt, inventory, receivables, ROE/ROIC, segment and
geographic mix, customer or IP concentration, management ownership, buybacks,
dividends and valuation multiples.

If a figure is unavailable, write "not disclosed" and continue. Never invent a
number, a quote or a date. Distinguish reported, adjusted and author-estimated
figures. Cite the source and period for every material number.

### 2. Decompose the business

Explain in one paragraph how the company earns cash. Split revenue into drivers
(volume, price, mix, geography, channel) and identify the unit economics.
Separate:

- **Stable core**: repeat or consumable demand, installed base, membership,
  essential use cases, recurring maintenance.
- **Cyclical layer**: channel restocking, macro, fashion, promotions, inventory
  cycle, currency.
- **Bubble layer**: speculative demand, resale premia, one-off events, channel
  stuffing, pull-forward from future periods.

This three-layer split is the core analytical move. State which layer is growing
or shrinking and what evidence supports the classification. A company can look
cheap on peak earnings and expensive on normalized earnings; the split prevents
that mistake.

### 3. Apply the three lenses

Read [the framework](references/framework.md) before scoring. For each lens,
record evidence for and against; do not average away contradictions.

- **Buffett**: circle of competence, moat, owner earnings, capital allocation,
  management integrity, margin of safety.
- **Duan Yongping**: 买股票就是买公司; business-model differentiation and pricing
  power; 本分/平常心/stop doing list; 毛估估; opportunity cost.
- **Munger**: inversion and pre-mortem; psychology and incentives; the "too hard"
  pile; the 100% buy test; avoid stupidity.

Score five dimensions from 1 to 5: business quality, moat, management, valuation,
risk (5 = best). Show the evidence behind each score. A score without a source is
an opinion.

### 4. Normalize earnings

Estimate mid-cycle owner earnings, not the latest peak or trough. Adjust for
one-offs, FX, stock-based compensation, channel stuffing, inventory build,
maintenance capex and working capital. Show the bridge from reported net profit
to normalized free cash flow. Use a range, not a point estimate. Explain every
adjustment and label it as reported, adjusted or estimated.

### 5. Value with scenarios

Build bear, base and bull cases with explicit revenue, margin and multiple
assumptions. Show implied market cap, per-share value and return from the current
price. Compute a rough expected value with stated probabilities. Cross-check with
a reverse DCF: what growth and margin does the current price imply?

Compare with at least one durable peer and with the opportunity cost of an index
or the user's best alternative. Do not present a single target price as fact.

### 6. Decide and define the rules

Choose one verdict:

- **BUY**: within your circle, moat durable, management trustworthy, price offers
  a margin of safety.
- **WATCH**: good business but price, evidence or a key uncertainty is not right.
- **PASS**: outside the circle, poor business, or a governance/accounting red flag.

State the buy zone, watch zone and avoid zone; position size; expected return;
and what would change the verdict. Define sell rules before buying.

### 7. Build the summary card

Write `summary.json` from the schema. Run:

```bash
python scripts/make_summary_card.py summary.json --out summary.png
```

The script uses Pillow for PNG. If Pillow is unavailable it writes an SVG and
prints a warning. Verify the output with an image read. If Chinese characters
render as boxes, pass `--font /path/to/CJK-font.ttf` or set `SUMMARY_CARD_FONT`.

The card must contain: company, ticker, as-of date, verdict, five scores, key
metrics, three-layer truth, bull and bear points, three lens lines and decision
zones. Keep bullets short; the script wraps text and shrinks it within limits, but
it does not rewrite content.

### 8. Deliver

Lead with the verdict and the one reason that matters. Then the evidence. Separate
fact, estimate and opinion. Include the image and the note. End with the tracking
checklist and "not investment advice."

## Failure handling

- Missing price or share count: mark valuation as unavailable; still produce the
  qualitative thesis and a card with "valuation: n/a".
- Missing image tooling: produce the SVG fallback; do not omit the image.
- Conflicting data: show both figures, cite dates, and state which one you use.
- User asks for a quick take: still produce the card, but shorten the note.
- User asks for short-term trading or a price prediction: decline that framing and
  offer the long-term thesis instead.

## Quality bar

- Every material claim has a source and a date.
- No invented numbers, quotes or management intentions.
- The bear case is as strong as the bull case.
- The verdict follows from the evidence, not from a personality or a narrative.
- The image is readable at chat size and contains no clipped text.
- The note distinguishes "I know", "I estimate" and "I guess".
